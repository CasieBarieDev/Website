Is the water warm enough? What do the latest quality measurements say? When can I go swimming? I built Zwemweer to bring those answers together in Home Assistant.


A compact dashboard badge shows the main status. Opening it reveals swimming advice, water conditions, and pool schedules in a custom interface for desktop and mobile.

{% include scroll-down.html target="swim-planning" %}

Before Heading Out

The outdoor overview brings water and air temperatures together with water quality, bacteria readings, and measurement dates. Seeing when a reading was taken is just as useful as seeing the reading itself.


On a cold day, I can check the outdoor advice and then switch to the pool schedule to find a suitable swim time. The schedule also highlights restrictions, such as swimming lessons that may affect lane availability.

Connected to Home Assistant

Zwemweer reads its measurements, advice, and schedule data from Home Assistant entities. Tapping a reading or status opens the associated entity details, keeping the underlying information within reach.


A sensor can provide the preferred location. If that value is unavailable, the card falls back to a choice based on the season and outdoor swimming advice. Missing measurements and schedule data older than three hours are shown explicitly.

Built for Both Screens

On desktop, the outdoor overview and pool schedule sit side by side. On mobile, they become swipeable tabs with smooth transitions. The popup keeps a consistent height, while the longer schedule scrolls independently.


  • A custom JavaScript web component uses Shadow DOM to keep the card’s styles contained.
  • The badge and detail view share the same status calculation to keep their advice consistent.
  • Container queries adapt the layout to the space available inside the dashboard.
  • Targeted DOM updates preserve existing elements as readings change.
  • Transitions respect the device’s reduced-motion preference.

I built Zwemweer for personal use and don’t plan to release it.
